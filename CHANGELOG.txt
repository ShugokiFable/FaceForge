## [0.23.2] - 2026-08-06 - correction: 0.23.0's slider gate was wrong

Agent: Claude
Parent: FaceForge 0.23.1

**0.23.0 and 0.23.1 dropped roughly 42 working sliders from every preset built on a
High Poly Head character. This release stops that.** User-reported: presets after 0.23.0 read
less accurate and more exaggerated than 0.22.0. They were right.

Three separate mistakes, all in the 0.23.0 morph registry:

  - **High Poly Head DOES ship EFM-compatible morphs**, contradicting what 0.23.0 measured
    and wrote into the docs. HPH registers KL\femalehead_efm.tri -- with cme, ece, rans,
    nuska, extra, expr and race -- from
    meshes\actors\character\facegenmorphs\high poly head.esm\morphs.ini, which is packed
    inside High Poly Head.bsa. Verified by parsing the archive directly.
  - **A morphs.ini line registers one base mesh against MANY extensions**, comma-separated.
    HPH puts nine on a single line. The 0.23.0 parser read the base and the first extension
    only, discarding the rest even where the file was loose.
  - **The registry read loose files only and still drew negative conclusions.** The 0.23.0
    safeguard ("a slider the registry has never heard of keeps its prior treatment") protected
    the wrong half: EFM's slider ini IS loose, so EFM sliders were judged against a morph set
    missing everything the archives supply.

Fixed:
  - Added FaceForge.Core/BsaIndex: parses the folder table of every installed .bsa (v104/v105)
    without extracting or decompressing, to detect archives that declare facegenmorphs content.
  - MorphRegistrySnapshot now carries Complete + UnreadArchives. On this install Complete is
    false and names 4 archives (High Poly Head, RaceMenu, ECE Sliders, AZ_HouseMaid).
  - resolveMorphAvailability returns null on an incomplete registry, so nothing is dropped.
    A partial view can prove a slider live; it can never prove one dead.
  - morphs.ini parser reads every extension on a line.
  - Reverted the wrong "correction" to hphCalibration's EFM note; the original text was right.

Behaviour: slider output returns to the 0.22.0 set. The inert-slider notice no longer fires on
this install.

Tests: 72 frontend (2 new, pinning the regression), 96 native assertions (4 new).

Nexus: YES -- supersedes 0.23.1, which should not stay up.

FaceForge 0.23.0 - 2026-08-05

*** loose files only and could not see High Poly Head's morph registrations inside its BSA.
*** The "51 EFM sliders are inert on HPH" claim and the hphCalibration correction are retracted.

- Started from a complete 0.22.0 snapshot.
- Fixed the defect behind "the preset looks right on the player but flat on a follower built from
  it": FaceForge was writing sliders that the target head cannot move. Measured on this
  installation, 51 of the EFM sliders it emits are inert on a High Poly Head character.
- Added FaceForge.Core/MorphRegistry: reads the installation's own
  meshes\actors\character\facegenmorphs tree -- morphs.ini extension registrations, races.ini
  per-race slider sets, and sliders\*.ini morph pairs -- then loads every chargen .tri the

  being installed. Inert sliders are left out of the preset instead of written as dead keys, and
  the analysis notice says how many were dropped and why.
- The rule only ever subtracts. A slider the registry has never heard of -- a slider ini shipped
  inside a BSA is invisible to a loose-file read -- keeps whatever the inventory decided, so
  "not found" can never silently delete a working slider.

  morphs. Expressive Facegen Morphs registers its extension against the vanilla
  Actors\Character\Character Assets head and ships 996-vertex morphs; the HPH head is 3832, and
  nothing on this install registers an EFM extension against the KL path.

  slider shaping into vertices. A bounded pair fit of the exported head against the full 122-morph
  vanilla set explains only 41.4% of its displacement (Inoue) and 42.1% (the known-good Silvia
  preset), so the extension morphs are present in the exported geometry. The jslot carries no
  vertex data; the exported NIF does.
- Confirmed FaceForge writes no tintInfo and no head-part `type` ordinal. Both come from whichever
  foundation preset the user loads, so the RGB(206,205,204) skin tone and the differing type
  ordinals in the reported preset are inherited, not generated.
- Added qa/measure-exported-head.py, the rig that produced those numbers: it parses
  BSDynamicTriShape vertices out of a FaceGeom NIF and fits them against a chargen .tri.
- 70 frontend tests (8 new), 92 native assertions (10 new), TypeScript, zero-warning .NET Release.

FaceForge 0.21.0 - 2026-07-31

- Dropped Elder from the offered races. ElderRace is the aged-human head Skyrim uses for old NPCs,
  not a race anyone creates a character as, and the app's own card said "No matching installed RACE
  record" while ranking it first for a young woman's photograph. Adding it in 0.20.0 was a
  regression: the table had eight races before, and measuring the morph file made it easy to include
  one nobody can select. A candidate the user cannot pick is not a recommendation.
- Rebased the mean playable head on the eight offered races rather than nine, so the reference head
  no longer averages in one nobody starts from. Under half a percent, but it was inconsistent with
  the reason Elder was removed.
- Rank races in measurement space instead of slider space. Going through the sliders puts every
  comparison through per-slider sensitivities and a clamp, tuned for describing one face rather than
  for telling near-identical heads apart -- and the measured morphs are near-identical. Under that
  metric a race's own head did not reliably rank it: Imperial's head, close to the average, came
  fourth. Mean relative distance per proportion is what "how far is this face from that head" means,
  and it makes the identity case exact. All eight now identify themselves.
- Weight the camera-facing half of a turned head instead of averaging both halves evenly. The
  reported photograph was turned 26.8 degrees, paired only 155 points and disagreed left-to-right by
  26%; its far half is foreshortened and partly hidden behind the nose and cheek, so a flat average
  mixed guesses into the good half at full strength. The weight stays at one half below eight
  degrees, where two good halves average better than one, and ramps to 0.85 by thirty. Which side
  faces the camera is read from the mesh -- the near half's features sit further from the symmetry
  axis -- not from a sign convention.
- Verified 62 frontend tests (was 61), 82 native assertions, clean builds, single-file gate, and an
  isolated one-file launch.

- Fixed the race ranking. Reported from a real run: a young woman's photo ranked Redguard joint-top

FaceForge 0.19.0 - 2026-07-31

- Added `TriFile`, a reader for the FaceGen `.tri` format (magic `FRTRI003`). FaceForge had never
  opened a mesh file; it reasoned about faces in 2D and estimated what each slider does. The
  geometry was on disk the whole time.
- This removes the blocker the roadmap put in front of sculpting. Sculpt deltas index the vertex
  array of a CharGen `.tri`, and that array -- the base head -- is inside the same file the preset
  already names as its sculpt host. Reading the head NIF, listed as blocker 1, is not required.
- Layout, verified byte-exactly: 64-byte header, then vertices (`numVerts*3` float32), triangles,
  UVs and per-triangle UV indices when the flag bit is set, then `numMorphs` records of
  `{uint32 nameLength; char name[]; float32 multiplier; int16 delta[numVerts*3]}`.
- `nameLength` counts the trailing NUL. Assuming it does not leaves one byte per morph in the
  stream, which does not fail -- it shifts every delta after the first morph into garbage. The
  size equation caught it before a line of parser was written: the vanilla and High Poly Head
  chargen heads both leave 1,240 bytes for 122 morph names whose text sums to 1,118.
- `TrailingBytes` makes a drifted parse loud. The blocks carry no length prefixes, so one wrong
  stride silently returns the wrong geometry; requiring the file to be consumed exactly is what
  turns that into a visible failure.
- Verified against five real installed files, all consumed exactly: vanilla `FemaleHeadChargen.tri`
  996 v / 122 morphs, `KL\High Poly Head\FemaleHeadChargen.tri` 3832 v / 122, `EyesFemaleChargen.tri`
  176 v / 35, `MouthHumanFChargen.tri` 141 v / 34, and EFM's `FemaleHead.tri` 996 v / 134.
  996 and 3832 are exactly the sculpt host vertex counts the reference presets declare.
- First measurements, replacing estimates. Base head extent (x, y, z) in mesh units: vanilla
  12.644 / 16.269 / 21.829, High Poly Head 12.377 / 15.985 / 21.756 -- within 2%, so HPH is a
  topology refinement rather than a differently proportioned head. EFM morph displacement at
  weight 1 spans 0.515 (`EFM_Chin_Up`) down to 0.0067 (`EFM_Eyes_Inner_Thin`); `EFM_Nose_Wide` is
  0.133, so the full +3 slider moves the nose about 3% of head width.
- Added `--tri <path>` to the Core test harness: vertex/triangle/morph counts, trailing bytes, base
  mesh extent, and every morph's displacement at weight 1. This is the measuring instrument the
  next step uses to replace the estimated baselines and response weights in `sliderCatalog.ts`.
- Found and not yet resolved: the only EFM head morph file on this installation is 996-vertex
  vanilla topology, and its base mesh extent matches the vanilla head exactly. No 3832-vertex
  High Poly Head EFM morph file is present, and the HPH chargen TRI carries only the 122 vanilla
  morphs. Whether EFM sliders move an HPH head here is unverified and bears on the 0.15.0-0.18.0
  HPH work.
- Measured the neutral head, replacing 32 of the 39 estimated `measurementBaselines`. A slider is
  the deviation of a face from the head it starts on, so an error in that reference is a constant
  bias on every export -- present even for a face that needs no correction at all. The old numbers
  were estimates carried since 0.6.0.
- The method is deliberately not "measure the mesh". A front render of each CharGen head at
  all-zero sliders is fed through the same detector and the same measurement code that reads a
  photograph, so both sides of the comparison come from identical arithmetic and any bias in that
  arithmetic cancels instead of accumulating. `qa/render-head.py` renders, `qa/calibrate-baselines.mjs`
  measures, `qa/baseline-calibration.json` records every number including the rejected ones.
- Four heads: vanilla and High Poly Head, female and male. Largest corrections: `noseBridgeWidth`
  0.24 -> 0.078, `eyeInnerCorner` 0.055 -> 0.011, `noseTipWidth` 0.16 -> 0.078, `eyeInnerHeight`
  0.22 -> 0.057, `lipFullness` 0.058 -> 0.116, `chinWidth` 0.24 -> 0.178, `faceAspect` 1.34 -> 1.081.
- Effect, on the one face whose right answer is known -- the neutral head itself, which must export
  nothing. Against the 0.18.0 baselines it exported 107 non-zero sliders, mean 0.99, ninetieth
  percentile 2.14, several pinned at the +3 limit. Against the measured baselines, over the sliders
  driven by a calibrated measurement, mean 0.14 and maximum 0.73, nothing pinned. Same defect class
  0.18.0 fixed for the HPH factors, except it was in the baseline table all along.
- Three things the render could not be trusted to answer, kept estimated and marked in source.
  Skyrim's eyebrows are a texture on a plain forehead slab and the iris is a texture on a plain
  sphere, so a render has neither; the detector still returns brow and iris landmarks at full
  confidence, placed on the brow ridge and the eyeball, measuring something real that is not what
  the photograph side measures. `lipGap` is near zero on a closed mouth and the four heads disagreed
  by 175% of the mean. `eyeTilt` is zero by definition.
- Two render details that changed the numbers rather than the picture. A bare head has no eyeballs,
  so the lids read as closed and the pipeline faded every eye measurement toward the baseline being
  calibrated -- circular. Compositing the eye mesh and drawing the iris the eye texture would draw
  cleared it. And a front orthographic render is not automatically a zero-nod face: the pipeline read
  5-13 degrees of nod on these meshes and un-foreshortened heights by up to 2.5%, a correction it
  would not apply to a photograph of someone facing the camera, so each head is pitched until the
  reported nod falls under a degree.
- Contaminated readings are recovered, not discarded. A distrusted measurement is reported as
  `baseline + (measured - baseline) * trust`, which is exactly invertible, so the eye family still
  yields the number the detector saw. Re-running the calibration against a build already carrying the
  calibrated values reproduces all 32 to the last digit, which is what says the recovery is a real
  measurement and not a fixed point of the fade. Two consecutive runs also agree exactly.
- Converted the per-race baselines from absolute proportions to multipliers of the universal table.
  They were absolute, on the old scale, and would have gone on overriding the measured baselines with
  1.34-era numbers -- so after this change, choosing a race would have been worse than choosing none.
  The conversion divides each old target by the old universal baseline, so the relative spread is
  unchanged. Imperial came out at 1.0 across the board: it had been the universal baseline all along.
- Removed the dead `baseline` field duplicated on all 109 slider definitions. It mirrored the old
  universal table, was unreachable behind a `??` that could never fire, and now contradicted the
  calibrated values it appeared to state.
- Added the regression that the existing neutral-face invariant could not catch: that test builds its
  face out of the baselines, so it passes on any table, right or wrong. The new one uses what the
  detector measured on the real neutral vanilla female head.
- Fixed the shipped version strings. The EXE had been stamped 0.6.0 since 0.6.0, so every release
  from 0.7.0 to 0.18.0 reports the wrong file and product version in Windows. Now 0.19.0.0.
- Verified 59 frontend tests (was 58), 82 native assertions (was 71), clean .NET build across Core,
  Tests and Desktop, clean frontend build, single-file publish gate, and an isolated one-file launch
  from a clean folder (window titled FaceForge, stopped cleanly).

Runtime status: tool-validated. In-game likeness against the recalibrated baselines is user-side.

FaceForge 0.18.0 - 2026-07-31

- Fixed the estimation regression introduced in 0.16.0/0.17.0. A face whose measurements already equal the reference head has no deviation to encode and must export zeros; it did not. On the High Poly Head path a perfectly neutral face exported 27 non-zero sliders peaking at 0.55, and 34 peaking at 1.53 once race, sex and style stacked. Every real face carried that bias on top of its actual deviation.
- Root cause: baseline multipliers were being used as a strength knob. The 0.16.0 comment stated it outright -- "Factor < 1.0 lowers baseline -> stronger slider for the same measurement". A baseline states what the reference head measures; a gain states how hard FaceForge pushes. Conflating them injects a constant offset into every measurement.
- Withdrew HPH_BASELINE_FACTORS (now empty) and reverted HPH_RESPONSE_GAIN to the 0.18 value established in 0.7.0 against the hand-authored preset distribution.
- The reasoning behind the raise does not survive checking. Vertex density does not change morph semantics: an EFM slider's effect is defined by the TRI morph it drives, and High Poly Head ships its own EFM-compatible morphs. Nothing measured shows a given EFM value moving an HPH head less than a vanilla one; the claim was inferred from author output. And author values are not photo measurements -- hand-authored presets sit high in the band because authors stylize deliberately, so matching their mean by raising gain reproduces authorial exaggeration rather than measuring a face.
- Kept the parts of 0.15.0-0.17.0 that are evidenced: High Poly Head detection and auto-selection of the best HPH head for the active race/sex, the head mesh as a first-class appearance tab, and the empty HPH sculpt host shells (D-004, no invented vertex deltas).
- Kept the opt-in sex and shape-style nudges. They have the same shape as the withdrawn factors but are off by default and explicitly chosen, so they are a stated preference rather than a hidden rescale of everyone's export.
- Added the invariant test that would have caught this: a neutral face exports zeros on every automatic path, and selecting a head mesh does not change any slider value.
- Race recommendation now ranks by the correction each race actually needs, running the real generator against every candidate and measuring the result over the proportions that race defines. The previous ranking scored an abstract distance with tolerance constants unrelated to the exported values, so a race could be recommended and then produce large corrections. Recommendation and output can no longer disagree.
- Verified: neutral face 0 non-zero on both vanilla and HPH paths; synthetic face returns to mean 0.536 / p90 1.23 / max 2.65, inside the hand-authored band (mean 0.48-1.11, p90 1.4-2.3, max 3.00); HPH and vanilla outputs now identical.
- Verified 58 frontend tests, 71 native assertions, clean package.

Runtime status: tool-validated; RaceMenu visual likeness remains user-side.

- Left/right differences are removed by mirror-averaging the landmark mesh. EFM sliders are bilateral, so a symmetric input is the only thing the output can represent and averaging both sides beats trusting the nearer one.
